# Design Philosophy

Systems that must remain reliable under uncertainty, noise, and adversarial information require more than fluent generation. They require structure that forces information to earn its place.

## Core Positions

**Hierarchy with verification**  
Flat multi-agent pools collapse into premature consensus. Explicit layers with mandatory verification contracts keep dissent visible and force claims to survive independent scrutiny before they propagate.

**Zero-trust at boundaries**  
Every handoff is a potential failure point. Treating the previous layer as incomplete or potentially compromised is basic engineering hygiene when operating against social platforms, incomplete data, or motivated sources.

**Bias detection is not optional**  
Volume and engagement are not truth. Specialized components that surface narrative architecture, cognitive bias, and framing effects belong in the critical path, not as optional post-processing.

**Systems must evaluate and improve themselves**  
Both large agent hierarchies and smaller quantitative pipelines contain explicit feedback and update mechanisms. A system that cannot measure its own error and adjust is unfinished.

**Physical and mathematical consistency are constraints**  
Where signals can be checked against conservation principles, ensemble statistics, or domain physics, they should be. Narrative environments especially benefit from hard external constraints.

**Documentation must be executable**  
Architecture and protocols are written so a capable implementer can turn them into running systems. Diagrams that cannot be executed have limited value.

## Origins

Significant formation occurred in high-precision physical domains — scientific glass, flameworking, casting, and related materials work. Those environments do not tolerate approximation. Process history, tolerances, and repeatability are non-negotiable. The same standard is applied here to software architecture and verification protocols.

The work in this repository is the practical expression of that standard.
